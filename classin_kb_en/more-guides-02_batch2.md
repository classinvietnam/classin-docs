## TI Store Tutorial

# I. Activating the TI Store Service

Product management and the TI Store are currently value-added services, available only to enterprise customers — please contact your dedicated account manager to enable them.

# II. Adding / Listing / Delisting Products in the Backend

Academic teachers in the backend can add products through the class's Product Management feature.

During class, all teachers, teaching assistants, and backend academic staff can only have 1 product listed at a time — if someone lists a new product, the previously listed one is automatically taken down from the live stream.

The backend's "livestream listing" and "playback listing" settings can be turned on or off at any time, but the livestream listing setting only takes effect during the livestream, and the playback listing only takes effect during the post-class playback period.

You can select existing products from the product catalog — each class session supports selecting up to 6 products at once.

# III. Listing / Delisting a Product in the Classroom

During a livestream, teachers or teaching assistants can go to "Teaching Tools → TI Store" in the ClassIn livestream classroom, or academic teachers can go to "Class Product Management Settings" in the backend, to set a product's status to "Live for Sale," letting students purchase it directly while watching the livestream.

# IV. Students Purchasing a Product

While watching a livestream or playback, students can purchase any product that's currently set to "Live for Sale" or "Playback for Sale."

## Temporarily Exiting Antivirus/Security Software

## Introduction

Why exit security software? Most antivirus and security software on the market today uses a certain amount of memory while running, and some security software may also throttle your computer's network connection, causing it to slow down. For users with lower-performance computers or unstable network connections, temporarily exiting security software can improve your computer's speed and network stability.

## Sunlogin Usage Guide

## I. Windows Computer

1.1 In your browser, go to https://sunlogin.oray.com/download, click Personal Edition, select Windows, and download the "Full 64-bit Version."

1.2 Once it's running, click the location shown to display the "local verification code," then provide the "local identification code" and "local verification code" to support staff.

## II. Mac Computer

2.1 In your browser, go to https://sunlogin.oray.com/download, click Personal Edition, select macOS, click "Download Now" below. You can check the download progress in the top-right corner of your browser; once done, double-click the installer.

2.2 In the window that pops up, double-click the installer, then click "Continue" in the window that appears and follow the steps to install.

2.3 Once installation is complete, open Sunlogin and click the exclamation mark next to "Allow Control of This Device" at the top.

2.4 In the access permission settings on the right, enable "Screen Recording" and "Accessibility." A Security & Privacy settings window will automatically pop up on the left — check the box for Sunlogin under both Accessibility and Screen Recording.

2.5 Provide the "local identification code" and "local verification code" to support staff.

# Frequently Asked Questions

1. Nothing happens after tapping the card

Check whether the institution information from step one has been set — institution information must be configured before binding a third-party NFC card.

Confirm you're using the latest version of the software.

If both of the above are fine, contact your hardware support colleague to confirm the card reader is working properly.

2. Which devices are supported?

In theory, any card or electronic device with an NFC chip should work.

3. After binding via phone NFC, it still prompts for QR-code binding verification

Phone NFC needs to be bound to a virtual card (transit card, bank card, electronic access card) to serve as the bound card. On Android, you can generally create a blank virtual card yourself.

If no card is bound, the phone's NFC module automatically generates a new temporary virtual card number each time, which is why it keeps prompting you to bind every time.

Introduction

Together with our User Agreement and Privacy Policy, this policy provides you with information about the cookies and other similar technologies used across all our websites. We review this policy from time to time to ensure it stays up to date.

If we make changes, we'll publish the latest version of the policy here. For significant changes, we'll notify you either the next time you visit our services or through other communication channels.

I. What are cookies, and why do we use them?

To ensure ClassIn runs properly, we store small text files called cookies on your computer or mobile device. A cookie typically contains an identifier, the site name, and some numbers and characters.

The information cookies collect lets us:

Tailor our websites to your individual needs.

Remember notifications already shown to you, so we don't show them again.

Improve and update our websites based on how you use them.

Note: we generally do not use cookies to identify who you are.

II. What kinds of cookies do we use?

We use the following categories of cookies:

Category 1: login cookies

Login cookies help us remember data such as your username, so you don't have to re-enter it on your next visit. All information these cookies collect is anonymous, and they cannot track your browsing activity on other websites.

Category 2: notification cookies

Notification cookies help us remember your setting choices, so you don't need to set them again on your next visit, and we won't repeat notifications you've already seen.

Category 3: functional cookies

Functional cookies collect information about how you use our websites, so we can improve them for you going forward. We also use the HMACCOUNT cookie to track visit counts and traffic sources, to measure and improve our website's performance. All information collected by these cookies is aggregated anonymously.
