## Dual-Teacher Scenarios and Cross-Disciplinary On-Site Teaching

## I. Scenario Overview

### Dual-Teacher Classroom

China's 2022 curriculum plan and curriculum standards brought cross-disciplinary courses and teaching to the forefront, setting clear requirements for schools and teachers. The preface to the curriculum plan and each subject's curriculum standards clearly lays out the main changes to the standards, especially the optimization of curriculum content structure; the curriculum plan's core principles emphasize strengthening the integrated nature of coursework; guidance on curriculum design and implementation stresses that comprehensive practical activities should focus on cross-disciplinary, inquiry-based learning and social practice; and curriculum standard development and textbook writing set clear class-hour ratio requirements for cross-disciplinary practice — in principle, every subject should devote no less than 10% of its class hours to cross-disciplinary themed teaching. In addition, many scenarios that need to break through spatial boundaries — cross-school teaching research, educational support programs, expert-teacher classrooms, emergency online classrooms during a pandemic, and more — can all be realized through the dual-teacher classroom.

ClassIn, an integrated teaching-and-learning digital platform, supports both teaching and teaching-research scenarios well. Beyond regular offline/online/OMO classrooms, when paired with the right hardware, it can effectively support synchronized dual-teacher classrooms, expert-teacher classrooms, emergency online classrooms during a pandemic, and support programs for remote regions. On the research side, it can meet teachers' needs for remote lesson observation and evaluation, joint teaching research, and group training.

## III. How to Put It into Practice

### Lesson Prep

Teachers can choose teaching resources from the Resource Center, or upload their own local teaching resources to the cloud drive, then open them in the classroom for teaching. Courseware supports online editing and online printing.

Teachers doing dual-teacher instruction can create a class session during lesson prep to communicate and co-create the lesson.

Beyond preparing the subject knowledge, they should also plan the interaction and communication between the two sides.

### Pre-Class Hardware and Software Setup

When setting up the dual-teacher class session, please set the teachers' roles as "teacher" and "co-teacher."

Please test your equipment ahead of time — once in the classroom, set up the front and rear cameras, microphones, and classroom layout (camera and microphone settings are remembered).

If using a mobile microphone, please make sure it's fully charged in advance.

Both teachers should enter the classroom ahead of time to test network conditions and confirm audio/video transmission is smooth (with good hardware and network conditions, ClassIn's audio/video technology achieves under 300ms of latency, ensuring smooth dual-teacher instruction).

Check that the materials needed for the lesson have already been uploaded to the cloud drive and finished transcoding, making sure you can smoothly pull up materials and operate tools, keeping the pace of the class smooth.

If using dual or triple displays, open Video Wall ahead of time and test what will be shown on each screen (in a dual-display setup, the reflection display shows the students from the paired class; in a triple-display setup, the reflection display shows the paired class's students, while the second front display shows the paired teacher).

If students are using smart devices, make sure the devices are charged, connected to the network, and logged into the correct accounts ahead of time; for any segment requiring students to operate their smart device, the teacher needs to authorize them.

Note: for your first time teaching in a dual-teacher scenario, we recommend seeking help from a consultant.

## V. Dual-Display/Triple-Display Scenarios

### Dual-Display Scenario

Building on a standard dual-teacher setup, adding a second screen as a "reflection display" — placed at the back or side of the classroom — creates a dual-display scenario.

Dual-display dual-teacher setups let the lead teacher face their own class while simultaneously seeing the remote class's students. When teachers are new to dual-teacher instruction, they're often not yet used to camera-facing presentation and keep wanting to turn around to check the remote class's camera feed on the large display. In a ClassIn-supported dual-teacher classroom, this is easily solved by enabling Video Wall to show the remote class's camera feed and placing that video wall on the reflection display — truly letting the two classes' students learn together as if in the same room, breaking through spatial limits.

### Triple-Display Scenario

Building on the dual-display scenario, adding a third screen at the front of the classroom — dedicated to showing the remote teacher's video window — helps teachers and students across locations make eye contact and share gestures, while also creating a sense of "being face-to-face," strengthening the emotional connection between teacher and students, truly achieving the feeling that "though far apart, we're as close as neighbors" in dual-teacher instruction.

Using lesson examples as a vehicle, Haidian Jinxiao Education Group carried out on-site teaching practice for the "dual-teacher classroom" and "integrated joint research" activities. **[Dual-Teacher Classroom]** Two teachers jointly prepared the lesson, co-taught, and interacted with each other, forming a "lead teacher + assisting teacher" practice partnership. Together they presented an 8th-grade biology lesson on-site, centered on "The Path to High-Quality, High-Yield Domestic Tomatoes — Applying Principles of Genetic Variation in Breeding," as part of a "dual-teacher classroom" on-site teaching practice.

**2. AI-Assisted Practice for "Dual-Teacher Classroom" and "Integrated Joint Research" — Elementary Chinese Language**

**[Dual-Teacher Classroom]** This activity combined "on-site dual-teacher classroom teaching practice + online/offline discussion + nationwide livestream," bringing together education peers from 18 provinces, autonomous regions, and municipalities across the country to watch the class online and take part in discussion — the livestream drew over 40,000 views. Teacher Du Chunhuan from Haidian Jinxiao-affiliated Experimental Primary School and Teacher Liu Caizhao from Haidian Jinxiao-affiliated Third Experimental Primary School served as the lead teachers, delivering a lesson on "Zhaozhou Bridge," centered on the theme "Learning Tasks That Promote Deep Interaction." Teachers and students from both schools broke through spatial boundaries, interacting, presenting, and sharing as if in the same room. Students engaged actively in learning, listened attentively to one another, proactively expressed their views, discussed enthusiastically, and completed the lesson's learning tasks to a high standard. Teacher Du took the lead in assigning classroom tasks, organizing activities, and providing guidance and demonstrations, while Teacher Liu provided timely organization, guidance, prompts, and personalized support for the students. The two teachers taught in close coordination, and their interaction with each other also helped drive interaction among the students. In addition, the online teaching platform's features — photographing and uploading work to display, zooming in to track and get close-ups of students, and smoothly switching between multiple video feeds — also strongly supported communication and sharing between the two schools' teachers and students.

Both teachers took a leading role, coordinating closely on assigning learning tasks, organizing activities, guiding their own students, and facilitating interaction between the two classes. In addition, pre-class assessment and analysis of learning status, tablet-based group learning, and the online teaching platform's features — same-screen writing, zooming in to track and get close-ups of students, and photographing and uploading work to display — also strongly supported communication and sharing between the two schools' teachers and students.
