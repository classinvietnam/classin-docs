## Managing Student Audio/Video

## I. Camera

## 1. Inviting a Student on Stage

### Applies to
Lead teacher, co-teacher(s).

### When to use this
During teaching, to encourage students to interact and share their answers, teachers can quickly invite a student on stage to answer a question using the roster tool. If the student has their camera enabled, their live camera feed will show once they're on stage — a more direct way to see the student's performance and answer.

### Usage Notes
Once the on-stage seats are full, inviting another student on stage from off-stage will bump the student who joined the stage earliest back off stage.

Students cannot be invited on stage in a 1v0 class type.

### Notes
In the classroom, the "lead teacher" or "co-teacher" can only control whether a student's camera feed is shown on stage — they cannot control whether the student's camera itself is turned on or off. Only the student can turn their own camera on or off.

### Steps

#### Computer
1. Open Roster on the right side of the classroom.
2. Find the student.
3. Click Go on Stage (click again to bring them off stage).

## 2. Sending a Student Off Stage

Once a student has finished answering on stage and there's no longer a need to show their camera feed, teachers can send the student off stage using the quick-action buttons below their camera. If a student is being disruptive while on stage, the teacher can similarly send them off stage.

Android phones and Android tablets do not have an "Off Stage" feature.

Up to four buttons can display below a student's camera. If "Off Stage" doesn't appear, go to "Change Buttons Below Student Camera" in the "Backend Management System."

#### Computer/iPhone/iPad
1. Click the student's camera.
2. Among the buttons below the camera, click Off Stage.

## 4. Swapping Students on Stage in One Click

Once a group of students has finished answering questions or presenting their work, teachers can use the "Swap Students on Stage" quick-action button to send all current on-stage students back off stage and invite the next group on — improving classroom efficiency.

Android phones and Android tablets do not have a "Swap Students on Stage" feature.

Up to four buttons can display below the teacher's camera. If "Swap Students on Stage" doesn't appear, go to "Change Buttons Below Teacher Camera" in the "Backend Management System."

1. Among the buttons below the camera, click Swap Students on Stage.

## 5. Rotation

If a teacher wants students' cameras to rotate through display on stage, they can enable the rotation feature in the roster.

Enabling rotation lets students take turns showing their camera on stage.

On mobile, only iOS phones and tablets support rotation (i.e. rotating students automatically onto the stage); Android and HarmonyOS phones/tablets do not have this feature.

1. Turn Rotation on/off in the bottom-right corner.

## 7. Auto-Invite on Raised Hand

Once a teacher enables "Auto-Invite on Raised Hand," any subsequent student who raises their hand will be automatically invited on stage by the system — without the teacher needing to do this manually.

iPhone/Android phones/Android tablets do not have an "Auto-Invite on Raised Hand" feature.

1. Tap the Raise Hand button in the bottom-right corner of the classroom.
2. Turn "Auto-Invite on Raised Hand" on or off.

## 8. Private Video Chat Between Teacher and Student

While teaching, if you need to address a specific student's issue privately — for example, to redirect a student who's being disruptive — a co-teacher can use the 1-on-1 private chat feature to temporarily pull themselves and that student into a separate room. In this room, only the teacher and the pulled-in student are present, which avoids disrupting the lead teacher's pacing. The lead teacher can also use this feature to communicate privately with a student and resolve something that needs to be handled one-on-one.

The private chat feature in the classroom is actually implemented via a breakout room. In the breakout room, you can use the same set of tools for communication and discussion. For details on how to use these tools, see the "Group Discussion" usage guide.

Phones and Android tablets cannot initiate a private chat invitation — they can only receive one sent by someone else.

Up to four buttons can display below a student's camera. If "Private Chat" doesn't appear, go to "Change Buttons Below Teacher Camera" in the "Backend Management System."

1. Among the buttons below the camera, click Private Chat.

## II. Microphone

## 1. Muting All Students' Microphones

If several students' microphones are producing interference noise while they aren't speaking, the teacher can mute all students' microphones to prevent it from disrupting the lesson or other students' experience.

If echo occurs during teaching and the teacher can't pinpoint which student is causing it, the teacher can mute all students' microphones to eliminate the interference and its impact on teaching.

**How to tell whether the teacher has enabled "Mute All Students" in the classroom:** check whether there's a red microphone icon in the bottom-right corner of the teacher's camera feed. If not present, the teacher hasn't enabled mute-all; if present, they have.

Android phones and Android tablets do not have a "Mute All" feature.

Up to four buttons can display below the teacher's camera. If "Mute All" doesn't appear, go to "Change Buttons Below Teacher Camera" in the "Backend Management System."

1. Among the buttons that pop up below the camera, click Mute All (click again to unmute all).

## 2. Muting a Student's Microphone

When there are many students in on-stage seats, teachers can mute an individual student's microphone to help maximize teaching effectiveness and maintain good classroom order.

If a student's microphone produces interference noise while they aren't speaking, the teacher can mute that student's microphone to prevent it from disrupting the lesson or other students' experience.

If echo occurs in the classroom, the teacher can mute the microphone of the student causing it, eliminating the interference and its impact on teaching.

If a student on stage makes an inappropriate remark or disrupts the lesson, the teacher can mute that student's microphone to maintain classroom order and a good teaching atmosphere.

Teachers using an Android phone or Android tablet, which don't have quick-action buttons below the classroom camera view, can mute a student's microphone from the roster instead.

If "Mute" doesn't appear among the buttons below a student's camera, go to "Change Buttons Below Student Camera" in the "Backend Management System."

1. Among the buttons below the camera, click Mute (click again to unmute).
