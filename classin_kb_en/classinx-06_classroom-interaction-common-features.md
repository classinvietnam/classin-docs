## ClassInX Classroom Interaction and Common Features

# I. Entering the Classroom

## Usage Notes
When there's a classroom you can enter (teachers can enter the formal classroom up to 20 minutes early, students up to 10 minutes early), a class notification pops up in the bottom-left corner. Users can also tap or swipe up in the bottom-left corner to open the notification center, then click the "Start Class" button to enter the classroom.

## Steps
1. Tap or swipe up in the bottom-left corner of the screen to open the notification center.
2. Click Start Class.

# II. Exiting the Classroom

While in the classroom, users can tap or swipe up at the bottom-center of the screen to open the dock, click the Settings button on the far right, then click "Exit Classroom" to leave the current classroom.

If it's not yet the formal class start time, users can also exit the current classroom from the notification center in the bottom-left corner. In addition, if the skin applied to the class's classroom settings doesn't allow the teacher to end class early, the lead teacher can still click Exit from the notification center in the bottom-left corner.

As long as it's not past the overtime period, exiting the classroom lets you re-enter to continue class. Once the overtime period is reached, you can no longer re-enter.

1. Tap or swipe up at the bottom-center of the screen to open the dock.
2. Click Settings on the far right.
3. Click Exit Classroom.

# III. Ending Class for Everyone

## Applies to
The lead teacher.

Provided the skin applied to the class's classroom settings has "Allow Teacher to End Class Early" checked, the lead teacher can, once the formal classroom is in session, tap or swipe up at the bottom-center of the screen to open the dock, go to Settings on the far right, then swipe "End Class for Everyone" to end the session early. Alternatively, they can click "End Class" from the notification center in the bottom-left corner.

Once "End Class for Everyone" is triggered, every user in the classroom is automatically removed, the classroom is closed, and neither teachers nor students can re-enter.

1. Swipe "End Class for Everyone."

# IV. Checking Class Session Elapsed Time

Once a user is in the classroom, they can use the "collapse" action in the notification center in the bottom-left corner to keep the notification dock pinned, showing the elapsed class time — for example, "01:05:06 elapsed." Once pinned, tapping or swiping up on the notification dock in the bottom-left corner again will make the elapsed-time display disappear.

1. Click the collapse button next to the notification center.

# V. Returning to the Desktop

Tap or swipe up at the bottom-center of the screen to open the dock, then click "Go to Desktop" to switch to the Windows system desktop.

While on the desktop, users can use the Click Tool from the radial dial to open, move, and perform other operations, or use the Pen tool to write on the desktop; once done, click the button below the radial dial to return to the whiteboard/classroom.

If a teacher clicks "Go to Desktop" while recording ClassIn Classroom or doing a local recording, the recording area switches to the entire Windows system desktop.

Also, in a formal classroom, if a teacher clicks "Go to Desktop," other users in the classroom cannot see what the teacher does on the desktop.

1. Click Go to Desktop.
2. Use the Click Tool to open/move files on the desktop, and so on.
3. Once done, click Return to go back to the whiteboard/classroom.

# VI. Classroom Chat Tool

Once inside the classroom, users can tap or swipe up at the bottom-center of the screen to open the dock, then go into the chat tool. The chat tool includes a Messages area and a Questions area.

In the Messages area, users can send text, emoji, and screenshots. Teachers can mute a student's chat access (a muted student cannot send messages or ask questions), and can copy, translate, or delete a student's chat message; if the message is an image, the teacher can also place that image onto the whiteboard for display.

In the Questions area, teachers can view and reply to questions raised by other users in the classroom.

In addition, teachers can also mute everyone or move the chat window off-screen from the settings below.

1. Click the Chat Tool.

# VII. Roster

## 1. Co-Teacher On-Stage/Off-Stage

### Usage Notes
By default, a co-teacher enters the classroom off-stage (camera not shown, cannot speak via microphone). If a co-teacher needs to go on stage (to show their camera and speak via microphone), they can click to go on stage themselves in the roster; if interaction isn't needed, they can click again to go off stage. Besides the co-teacher operating this themselves, the lead teacher can also send the co-teacher on or off stage from the roster.

In the roster, the on-stage button next to the co-teacher's name shows green when they're on stage, and gray when they're off stage.

### Steps
1. Click Roster.
2. Toggle the co-teacher's on-stage/off-stage status next to their name.

## 2. Student On-Stage/Off-Stage

In the classroom, the lead teacher or co-teacher can send students on or off stage from the roster. If a student is already on stage, the lead teacher or co-teacher can also click the student's on-stage camera and click the off-stage button in the tools below the camera to send that student off stage.

If the teacher wants every on-stage student sent off stage at once, they can click their own on-stage camera and click the "Send All Off Stage" button in the tools below their camera. The teacher's camera tools also include "Swap On-Stage Students," a related on/off-stage tool.

A student being on stage means they can show their camera and speak via microphone. A student being off stage means they're still in the classroom, but their camera isn't shown and they can't speak via microphone.

In the roster, the on-stage button next to a student's name shows green when they're on stage, and gray when they're off stage.

1. Toggle a student's on-stage/off-stage status next to their name.

## 3. Authorizing/Revoking a Student's Authorization

In the classroom, the lead teacher or co-teacher can authorize or revoke a student's authorization from the roster. If the student is on stage, the lead teacher or co-teacher can also click the student's on-stage camera and click the authorize button in the tools below the camera to authorize or revoke authorization.

If the teacher wants every on-stage student authorized, every on-stage student's authorization revoked, or students automatically authorized once they go on stage, the teacher can click their own on-stage camera and click the corresponding button in the tools below their camera.

An authorized student can use certain classroom tools, such as the pen, text, eraser, and operating the whiteboard.

Once authorized, a student's authorize button shows yellow; if the student is on stage, a yellow crown icon also appears in the top-right corner of their camera, indicating authorized status. Conversely, an unauthorized student's authorize icon shows gray, and no crown icon appears on their camera while on stage.

1. Toggle authorization/revoke authorization for a student next to their name.

## 4. Rewarding/Un-Rewarding a Student

In the classroom, the lead teacher or co-teacher can award a student a trophy from the roster as a reward, and the trophy count that student has earned this session also shows next to the trophy button. If the student is on stage, the lead teacher or co-teacher can also click the student's on-stage camera and click the trophy button in the tools below the camera to award them a trophy, with the trophy count also shown in the bottom-right corner of the student's camera.

If the teacher needs to undo a student's reward, the student must be on stage, then the teacher can press and hold the trophy icon in the tools below that student's camera to remove one trophy (press and hold on a large display; right-click with the mouse on computer).

Also, if the teacher wants to award every on-stage student a trophy, they can click their own on-stage camera and click the trophy button in the tools below their camera.

If the teacher needs to reward every student, they can use the Answer Clicker tool from the toolbox — once students respond, the teacher can recall the answer clicker to award every participating student a trophy.

1. Click the reward button next to a student's name to award them a trophy.
(While the student is on stage, pressing and holding/clicking the reward button in the tools below their camera removes a trophy.)

## 5. Muting/Unmuting a Student's Microphone

In the classroom, the lead teacher or co-teacher can mute or unmute a student's microphone from the roster.

If the student is on stage, the lead teacher or co-teacher can also click the student's on-stage camera and click the mute button in the tools below the camera to mute or unmute that student's microphone.

A student's microphone shows green when it's on and they can speak normally; once a teacher mutes them, their microphone turns red and they can no longer speak via microphone.

If the teacher needs to mute every student's microphone, they can click their own on-stage camera and click the mute button in the tools below their camera to mute or unmute every student's microphone at once. A red microphone icon in the bottom-right corner of the teacher's own on-stage camera indicates they've enabled mute-all.

1. Click "···" next to a student's name, then click Mute/Unmute Student's Microphone.

## 6. Private Chat with a Student via the Roster

In the classroom, if the lead teacher or co-teacher needs to communicate privately with a student, they can start a private chat from the roster. If the student is on stage, the lead teacher or co-teacher can also click the student's on-stage camera and click the private chat button in the tools below the camera. Besides these two ways to start a private chat, teachers using the Video Wall tool can also start a private chat with a student from below their camera in the video wall.

Clicking the private chat button quickly pulls the teacher and that student into a group discussion classroom, while other students remain unaffected in the main classroom. Once the teacher and student finish communicating, the teacher can click Group Discussion in the dock below, then click "End" in the bottom-right corner of the group discussion window — this returns the teacher and student to the main classroom and closes the group discussion classroom.

Note: if the teacher is recording class, the private chat will also be captured in the recording.

1. Click "···" next to a student's name, then click Private Chat.
2. Once the conversation ends, click Group Discussion in the dock right below.
3. Click End in the bottom-right corner of the group discussion window.

## 7. Muting/Unmuting a Student in Chat via the Roster (Chat Tool)

In the classroom, besides muting or unmuting a specific student's chat access within the classroom chat tool, the lead teacher or co-teacher can also do this from the roster.

A muted student cannot send messages or ask questions in the classroom chat tool.

1. Click "···" next to a student's name, then click Mute Chat.

## 8. Removing a Student from the Classroom

Provided the skin applied to the class's classroom settings doesn't have "Hide Remove Student Setting" checked, the class session's lead teacher or co-teacher can remove a student from the classroom via the roster.

The removal duration can be set — the default is 5 minutes, and you can adjust the duration after clicking the remove button, with a maximum of the entire class session and a minimum of 1 minute. If set to 5 minutes, the student can only click "Start Class" to re-enter the classroom after 5 minutes have passed.

Note: once the classroom enters the overtime period, students cannot enter the classroom.

1. Click "···" next to a student's name, then click Remove from Classroom.
2. Set the removal duration in the system prompt, then click Confirm.

## 9. Raise Hand to Auto-Go-On-Stage

In the classroom, when an off-stage student needs to speak with the teacher, besides the teacher sending the student on/off stage via the roster, the teacher can also enable "Raise Hand to Auto-Go-On-Stage" in the settings in the bottom-left corner of the roster. Once enabled, students can tap or press and hold the raise-hand button in the bottom-right corner of the classroom to raise their hand, and they'll automatically go on stage.

Note: if a classroom has many students, many students raising their hands at once could cause frequent, disruptive on-stage/off-stage switching, affecting teaching quality.

1. Click Settings in the bottom-left corner.
2. Turn "Raise Hand to Auto-Go-On-Stage" on or off.

## 10. On-Stage Seat Rotation

In the classroom, once the on-stage seats area is shown, the lead teacher or co-teacher can enable on-stage seat rotation from the roster, letting students take turns going on stage to show their camera and interact.

Once enabled, you can set the rotation scope (online students / students with camera on), rotation order (sequential/random), and rotation interval (in seconds).

If the teacher needs to interact with a specific student for an extended time and doesn't want them rotated off, they can drag that student's camera onto the whiteboard and adjust its position there.

1. Turn On-Stage Seat Rotation on or off (once on, set the rotation scope/order/interval).

# VIII. ClassInX Opening/Uploading Files (Courseware)

If a user needs to open a file from Cloud Drive/Space, or upload a desktop file into the classroom, they can tap or swipe up at the bottom-center of the screen to open the dock, click "Files," then go into Cloud Drive/Space to do this. Once opened, the file displays on the whiteboard.

In the classroom, only courseware opened via "Files" in the dock by the lead teacher or co-teacher can be seen by other users in the classroom.

1. Click Files.

# IX. Toolbox

Users can tap or swipe up at the bottom-center of the screen to open the dock, then go into the "Toolbox" to use more teaching tools, such as Small Blackboard, Countdown Timer, Browser, Answer Clicker, and more.

Also, some teaching tools are only usable in a formal classroom, such as Group Discussion and Livestream Chat.

1. Click Toolbox.

# X. Classroom LMS

Besides viewing and using a class's LMS activities within the class itself, teachers can also open and use them within the classroom. Once inside the classroom, teachers can tap or swipe up at the bottom-center of the screen to open the dock, then go into "LMS" to view the class's activities — what the teacher sees here stays in sync with what students see.

Teachers can also create and publish quizzes and answer sheet activities from the classroom's LMS. Once published, students in the classroom can answer directly within the classroom, and the teacher can go over the answers. In addition, teachers can create and publish assignment activities; unlike quizzes and answer sheets, students cannot answer or submit assignments within the classroom — they can only view and submit them later within the ClassIn class.

1. Click LMS.
2. Teacher views or creates an activity (quiz/answer sheet/assignment activity).

# XI. Entering the ClassIn Client

Users can tap or swipe up at the bottom-center of the screen to open the dock, then click "ClassIn" to enter the ClassIn interface, where they can view and use features outside the classroom, such as creating a class/classroom.

1. Click ClassIn.

# XII. ClassInX Screen Projection

When a teacher or student wants to display content from their phone/tablet or another computer in the classroom, they can tap or swipe up at the bottom-center of the screen to open the dock and go into "Screen Projection," or click "Screen Projection" in the toolbox. For projection to work, the projecting device and the receiving device must be on the same network.

**(1)** To project from another computer into the classroom, the projecting device needs to log into ClassIn, click Screen Projection in the left navigation, enter the receiving device's projection code and click Project, or select the receiving device's name to complete projection. The four green corners on the projecting device's screen indicate the projection area, which cannot be adjusted — the entire screen is always used.

**(2)** An Apple phone, tablet, or computer can project using the device's built-in screen mirroring feature, selecting the receiving device's name to complete projection; you can also download the ClassIn Screen Projection app from the App Store, entering the receiving device's projection code in the app and clicking Project, or selecting the receiving device's name to complete projection.

**(3)** An Android phone or tablet needs to download the ClassIn Screen Projection app from the app store, entering the receiving device's projection code in the app and clicking Project, or selecting the receiving device's name to complete projection.

**(4)** Other video apps with a built-in projection feature can also project their content into the classroom. Open the video app, play the video, then click the app's own projection button and select the receiving device's name to complete projection.

While using the screen projection tool, the bottom-right corner of the projection tool window offers more settings for this projection session:

**Sharing settings:**

Play the projecting device's sound through this device's speaker; share the projecting device's sound with everyone else in the classroom.

**Privacy settings:**

Allow receiving projections from all devices; once the projection window is open, allow receiving projections from all devices.

In addition, multiple devices can project into the classroom at once — once multiple devices have projected successfully, the receiving device can switch between different devices in the projection tool window's bottom-right corner to view different projected content.

1. Click Screen Projection, or go into the toolbox and click Screen Projection.

# XIII. Classroom Network/CPU Status

Once inside the classroom, users can tap or swipe up at the bottom-center of the screen to open the dock, then click the network icon to check current network and CPU status. When this icon turns orange or red, it means the network connection is poor or CPU usage is too high.

1. Click the network icon.

# XIV. ClassInX OMO Livestream Broadcast

## Prerequisites
Backend administrators need to enable the "OMO Livestream Broadcast" feature under School Settings → Value-Added Services in the backend, then check "OMO Livestream Broadcast" when creating the class session.

## When to Use This
When students only need to watch the teacher's livestream feed within the classroom and recording isn't needed, we recommend checking "OMO Livestream Broadcast" when creating the class session.

OMO Livestream Broadcast is a high-definition on-site recording mode — once the lead teacher selects the camera feed to display in the OMO livestream broadcast settings, online students in the classroom can open the OMO livestream broadcast window in the bottom-right corner of the classroom for a clearer view of the teacher's livestream instruction.

The lead teacher can also disable OMO Livestream Broadcast mid-class; once disabled, online students in the classroom will no longer see the livestream feed.

## Notes
When Record On-Site and OMO Livestream Broadcast are both enabled at the same time, they cannot each use a different camera — that is, when the lead teacher selects a camera for Record On-Site, OMO Livestream Broadcast automatically switches to that same camera, and vice versa.

Record On-Site and OMO Livestream Broadcast are only supported when the lead teacher uses a computer or large display (Windows) — other devices (such as phone/tablet) are not supported.

1. Enter the classroom.
2. Click OMO Livestream Broadcast in the dock right below.
3. Select a camera in the settings in the top-right corner of the OMO livestream broadcast window, then click Done; view the livestream feed.
4. Minimize the OMO livestream broadcast window.

# XV. ClassInX Class Extension

Provided the skin applied to the class's classroom settings has "Allow Teacher to Extend Class Duration" checked, the lead teacher or co-teacher can, within 8 to 3 minutes before class ends, extend class duration by 15 or 30 minutes (multiple extensions are allowed, but total class duration cannot exceed 24 hours; billing follows the extended class duration).

Once the extension pop-up appears, if the teacher hasn't decided yet, they can click "Think About It" — as long as it's still within the allowed extension window, the teacher can later click "Extend" from the notification center in the bottom-left corner of the classroom, then choose to extend by 15 or 30 minutes.

# XVI. AI "Material Search"

When a teacher uses ClassIn X software on a ClassInX 2.0 large display, they can use the AI "Material Search" (i.e., AI image search) agent to enrich their teaching. This agent combines AI semantic understanding, multimodal recognition, and a curated official image library, offering teachers a one-stop, intelligent way to search for and display teaching materials.

**Instant in-class retrieval:** seamlessly find and use image materials while teaching, with direct insertion onto the whiteboard.

**Intelligent search:** quickly locate needed materials via natural language, voice, image, and other multimodal input methods.

**High-quality resource aggregation:** integrates ClassIn's official curated library.

Once a teacher selects a text/image element on the whiteboard, they can click the AI button in the secondary editing tools and choose the "Material Search" agent to use it; if this agent hasn't been added yet, first click the "+" in the AI button, then search for and add it in AgentIn. In the agent's dialog box, click or press and hold to start voice input; you can also click "+" to upload a local image or screenshot — only one image can be sent at a time. Clicking an AI-generated material image inserts it onto the whiteboard, where you can select the image to adjust its position, size, and so on. You can also invoke this agent through the DaKe voice assistant.

Besides using this agent via the secondary editing tools on the whiteboard, you can also add and use it from the AgentIn agent store in the dock in the bottom-right corner of the whiteboard or classroom.

# XVII. AI "Function Graph"

When a teacher uses ClassIn X software on a ClassInX 2.0 large display, they can use the AI "Function Graph" agent, which directly generates function graphs, helping teachers make classroom demonstrations more visual, making abstract function teaching more efficient and easier for students to understand.

Once a teacher selects a function-related text/image element on the whiteboard, they can click the AI button in the secondary editing tools and choose the "Function Graph" agent to use it. The agent's dialog box supports uploading a local image or screenshot — only one image can be sent at a time. Clicking an AI-generated function graph inserts it onto the whiteboard, where you can select the graph to adjust its position, size, and so on. You can also invoke this agent through the DaKe voice assistant.

# XVIII. AI "Chinese Character Stroke Order"

When a teacher uses ClassIn X software on a ClassInX 2.0 large display, they can use the AI "Chinese Character Stroke Order" agent, which accurately recognizes one or more Chinese characters, automatically breaks down the character's stroke structure, and shows the standard writing order, helping teachers teach Chinese character writing more efficiently and visually.

Once a teacher selects a Chinese-character text/image element on the whiteboard, they can click the AI button in the secondary editing tools and choose the "Chinese Character Stroke Order" agent to use it. The agent's dialog box supports uploading a local image or screenshot — only one image can be sent at a time. Clicking an AI-generated stroke-order image inserts it onto the whiteboard, where you can select the image to adjust its position, size, and so on. You can also invoke this agent through the DaKe voice assistant.

# XIX. Agents in the Dock

When a teacher uses ClassInX software on a ClassInX 2.0 large display, the AgentIn agent store in the dock in the bottom-right corner of the whiteboard or classroom supports adding and using agents to assist teaching and enrich teaching scenarios.

When using an agent, the default input method is voice — click or press and hold to start voice input; you can also click "+" to upload a local file or image, making classroom interaction smarter.

If you no longer need a given agent, press and hold its icon in the bottom-right corner to remove it.

# XX. Tool Dial (Radial Menu)

ClassInX's tool dial includes commonly used teaching tools, such as the Pen, Text Tool, Eraser, Select/Move, Click Tool, Screenshot, Save/Share Whiteboard, Scan-to-Upload Image, and Undo/Redo.

If a user doesn't interact with the tool dial for a while, it automatically shrinks to a minimized state; clicking it again restores it to its normal (expanded) state. While expanded, clicking the center of the dial also switches it back to minimized state.
