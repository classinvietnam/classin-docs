## Classroom Settings

## I. Classroom Mode

## What is Classroom Mode?
"Classroom Mode" is a commonly used classroom setting. There are two modes: "Standard Mode," suited to purely online teaching, offering a clearer and more complete learning experience; and "Big Screen Mode," better suited to blended in-person/online teaching or touchscreen computers, offering a more intuitive and convenient way for teachers to operate.

## Applies to
Everyone.

## Usage Notes
On computer, ClassIn offers two interface styles for the classroom: "Standard Mode" and "Big Screen Mode."

In "Standard Mode," teaching tools sit on the right side of the classroom interface, and classroom settings sit in the top-right corner.

In "Big Screen Mode," teaching tools appear as a radial dial tool on the whiteboard, and classroom settings move to the bottom-left corner. On a touchscreen computer, you can four-finger-touch anywhere on the whiteboard to summon the radial tool to that location.

In the classroom, each user can set their own classroom mode; this setting only affects that user and doesn't affect anyone else in the classroom.

Switching classroom mode is equivalent to leaving the current classroom and re-entering. If a teacher switches classroom mode while recording, the recording will stop; once the switch is complete, the teacher needs to click Record again, and so on.

Besides switching within the classroom, users on computer can also switch mode from the local whiteboard, temporary classroom, or preparation room.

## Notes
"Classroom Mode" cannot be changed on phone or tablet.

## II. Showing/Hiding the On-Stage Seats Area

## What is the classroom's on-stage seats area?
The on-stage seats area is the row at the very top of the whiteboard. It's used only to display teacher and student camera feeds — you cannot write on it.

"Lead teacher" and "co-teacher."

Besides choosing whether to show the on-stage seats area when creating a class session, once inside the classroom, the lead teacher or co-teacher can also show or hide the on-stage seats area under the classroom's More settings.

When the classroom shows the "on-stage seats area," a row of camera feeds appears at the top of the classroom, including the "teacher" and any "students the teacher has invited on stage." Even if the "teacher" or those "students" haven't turned on their own camera, their camera tile will still appear in the row.

When the classroom hides the "on-stage seats area," no camera feeds display at the top of the classroom (including the teacher's), and the entire interface becomes the whiteboard, which can be used for writing and similar actions, or for showing courseware full-screen. In this state, other classroom members can still hear the lead teacher speaking; if the lead teacher wants to show their own camera, they can quickly turn it on from the top-right corner of the classroom, and it will appear in the top-left area of the classroom. To show a student's camera, the teacher needs to invite the student on stage from the roster.

Both co-teachers and the lead teacher have permission to hide the on-stage seats area. Once either one hides it, the on-stage seats area disappears from every other teacher's and student's screen in the classroom.

Also, the teacher's on-stage seat rotation setting only takes effect while the on-stage seats area is shown.

On phone or tablet, this feature is called "Camera Area."

## III. Focus Mode

## What is Focus Mode?
"Focus Mode" is a commonly used classroom setting. It's a classroom-management feature ClassIn designed specifically for classroom discipline, effectively preventing students from switching away from the classroom to play games, browse the web, or similar.

Backend administrators can apply a skin with "Enable Focus Mode" checked to a class, and formal classrooms under that class will have Focus Mode enabled by default. In addition, the lead teacher or co-teacher can also turn Focus Mode on or off under the classroom's More settings once inside the classroom.

Once Focus Mode is enabled: if a student enters the classroom on a Windows computer, the classroom window will stay maximized and pinned on top, unobstructed by other windows. If a student enters the classroom on a phone or tablet, switching to another app will automatically exit the classroom.

Focus Mode set by the lead teacher or co-teacher within the classroom only applies to the current classroom.

Only teachers using a computer or iPad can turn Focus Mode on or off in the classroom.

Focus Mode does not take effect for students using a Mac.

Focus Mode does not take effect for auditors.

If a student's device supports split-screen or similar features, Focus Mode may also fail to take effect.

1. Turn Focus Mode on or off.

## IV. Raise Hand Sound Alert

## When to use this
When a student raises their hand in the classroom, the teacher's side automatically plays a raise-hand alert sound, so the teacher can still notice a student's raised hand while focused on teaching, writing on the board, or viewing courseware, and won't miss a chance to interact.

Once inside the classroom, the lead teacher and co-teacher can each set their own raise-hand alert sound independently, with no effect on each other.

In raise-hand alert settings, teachers can choose no alert sound, or select a "ding," "tick-tock," or "ding-dong" sound as the alert.

The alert sound is only audible to the teacher themself — students in the classroom won't hear it.

Only teachers using a computer can set "Raise Hand Sound Alert" in the classroom.

1. Select a Raise-Hand Alert Sound.

## V. Entry/Exit Notifications

Once inside the classroom, if the teacher enables entry/exit notifications in classroom settings, a pop-up notification will appear on the teacher's screen whenever a student joins or leaves the classroom, helping the teacher track attendance status in real time. (In Standard Mode, this notification appears next to the roster on the right; in Big Screen Mode, it appears in the bottom-left corner.)

The lead teacher and co-teacher can each set their own entry/exit notifications independently, with no effect on each other.

If the lead teacher has this feature enabled, they'll also be notified when a co-teacher joins or leaves.

Only teachers using a computer can set entry/exit notifications in the classroom.

1. Turn entry/exit notifications on or off.

## VI. Changing the Classroom Background

A classroom background can be set in advance in the Backend Management System; once inside the classroom, teachers can also set it themselves under the classroom's More settings. Once a teacher changes the classroom background, the new one takes effect immediately.

Teachers can choose from the system's built-in background images, or upload a local image as the classroom background. Uploaded images must be JPG format and no larger than 1MB.

A classroom background changed within classroom settings only applies to the current classroom.

Only teachers using a computer can set the classroom background in the classroom.

1. Go to Classroom Background.
2. Select or upload a classroom background image.

## VII. Window Layout

Dragging cameras, courseware, and widgets to preset positions with automatic resizing helps teachers quickly organize the classroom whiteboard interface, efficiently switch teaching scenarios, and stay focused on instruction and interaction.

Once inside the classroom, teachers can go to Window Layout under classroom settings to choose an existing layout template. Custom layout templates are also supported — you can add a template using either tiled mode (pane size and position are fixed and cannot be freely adjusted) or free-pane mode (pane size and position can be freely adjusted).

Once you've selected a template, drag a camera, courseware, or widget with the mouse while holding Shift on the keyboard to enter layout mode. In Big Screen Mode on a touchscreen, dragging a camera/courseware/widget with one finger and holding for 0.5 seconds also enters layout mode.

Only teachers using a computer can set the window layout in the classroom.

1. Click More in the top-right corner of the classroom.
2. Click Window Layout.
3. Select or add a layout template.
4. Drag a camera/courseware/widget with the left mouse button while holding Shift on the keyboard; once the yellow layout frame appears, you're in layout mode.

## VIII. Quick Help Request

"Lead teacher," "co-teacher," "student" (excluding auditors).

When a teacher or student in the classroom runs into an issue they can't resolve on their own, they can request assistance from the academic administrator in the "Backend Management System."

Once inside the classroom, teachers can check off the relevant teacher(s)/student(s) involved under Quick Help Request, describe the issue, and submit it. The academic administrator can view the issue details, an automatically captured screenshot from the teacher's/student's perspective, and the tagged individual(s) under classroom monitoring in the backend. Once the administrator replies, a reply window will pop up for the teacher in the classroom.

Once the class has a custom skin applied that includes the "Allow Students to Send Help Requests to the School Backend" setting, students in the classroom can also use the Quick Help Request feature on iPad or computer. Students describe the issue and submit it; the academic administrator can view the help request content and an automatically captured screenshot from the student's perspective under classroom monitoring in the backend. Once the administrator replies, a reply window will pop up for that student in the classroom.

Sending a help request is not currently supported on phone or Android tablet.

## IX. Pinning the Classroom Window

## What is pinning the classroom window?
Pinning the classroom window is a commonly used classroom setting. Computer users who want the ClassIn classroom window to always stay unobstructed by other software can pin the classroom window.

Users entering the classroom on computer, in Standard Mode, can click Pin under More in the top-right corner; once pinned, the classroom window will by default display above every other app; clicking again unpins it. In Big Screen Mode, the classroom window is full-screen by default, so this feature doesn't apply.

Phone and tablet cannot pin the classroom window — it's full-screen by default.

1. Click Pin or Unpin.

## X. Leaving the Classroom

For users entering the classroom on computer: in Standard Mode, click the "X" in the top-right corner of the classroom window to leave the current classroom; in Big Screen Mode, click "Exit" under More in the bottom-left corner. On phone or tablet, tap "Leave Classroom" under the Settings button.

For the lead teacher entering the classroom on computer: in Standard Mode, after clicking the "X" in the top-right corner of the classroom window, you also need to click "Leave (Just Me)" to actually leave the current classroom; in Big Screen Mode, click "Exit" under More in the bottom-left corner. On phone or tablet, first tap the Settings button, then select "Leave (Just Me)."

As long as you leave before the grace period for running over, you can re-enter the classroom for class. Once the grace period begins, you can no longer re-enter.

## XI. Ending Class for Everyone

"Lead teacher."

### When to use this
If a teacher finishes covering the lesson content earlier than expected, they can use "End Class for Everyone" in the classroom to end the class session early.

Provided the class skin applied to the classroom setting has "Allow Teachers to End Class Early" checked, once the lead teacher has started the formal classroom, they can click "End Class for Everyone" to end the class session early.

Once "End Class for Everyone" is triggered, all users in the classroom are automatically removed, the classroom is closed, and neither teachers nor students can re-enter it.

## XII. OMO Livestream Broadcast

## Prerequisites
A backend administrator must first enable the OMO livestream broadcast feature under School Settings → Value-Added Services in the backend, and then check "OMO Livestream Broadcast" when creating the class session.

If online students only need to watch the teacher's livestream broadcast feed within the classroom, without needing cloud recording, we recommend checking "OMO Livestream Broadcast" when creating the class session.

OMO livestream broadcast mode is a high-definition mode for recording the live scene. Once the lead teacher selects which camera feed to display in the OMO livestream broadcast settings, online students in the classroom can open the OMO livestream broadcast window in the bottom-right corner to watch the teacher's on-site broadcast more clearly. The lead teacher can also turn off OMO livestream broadcast mid-class; once turned off, online students in the classroom will no longer be able to see the broadcast feed.

When on-site recording mode and OMO livestream broadcast mode are both enabled, they cannot use different cameras separately — when the lead teacher selects a camera in on-site recording mode, OMO livestream broadcast automatically switches to that same camera, and vice versa.

On-site recording and OMO livestream broadcast are only supported for lead teachers using a computer or big screen (Windows) — other devices (such as phone/tablet) don't support them.

1. Enter the classroom.
2. Click OMO Livestream Broadcast in the top-right corner (bottom-left corner in Big Screen Mode).
3. In the settings at the top-right of the OMO livestream broadcast window, select a camera, then click Done; view the broadcast feed.
4. Shrink the OMO livestream broadcast window.
