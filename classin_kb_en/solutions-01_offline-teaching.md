## Offline Teaching

Traditional offline teaching emphasizes knowledge and skills instruction, training students' problem-solving efficiency and ability. Building on this, by using learning task sheets and scaffolding to shape how lectures support student learning, and by arranging better instruction based on process-level data analysis from assignments, quizzes, or in-class teaching, lectures can evolve based on data-driven experience. Running idea generation alongside knowledge and skills instruction, and applying learning task sheets for precision teaching, opens up new possibilities for traditional offline teaching. ClassIn X/ClassIn is an education platform built for offline teaching scenarios, connecting lesson prep with pre-class, in-class, and post-class implementation, and connecting all content and data together — letting it fully coordinate and support every element and process involved in precision teaching.

## I. Lesson Prep

Teachers can choose teaching resources from the Resource Center, or upload their own local teaching resources to the cloud drive, then open them in the classroom for teaching. Courseware supports online editing and online printing.

### 2.2 Real Teaching Case Studies

**Beijing No.1 Normal University Affiliated Primary School — "Classification and Organization"**

Teacher Gao used ClassIn's whiteboard layer feature to turn shapes of different colors and forms into movable images, guiding students to freely drag the images to learn about classification and grouping concepts. Since the teacher didn't specify a classification standard, students' dragging actions ended up visualizing their own thinking.

The teacher used ClassIn's split-screen feature to show the whiteboard and PPT together — first guiding students to count different fruits on the whiteboard, then using the PPT to have students trigger the matching fruit count and write the answer in Arabic numerals. Using the same teaching materials across different classes, the teacher got a variety of randomly generated student responses.

**Beijing Xinxian Hutong Primary School — "Bringing in the New Year"**

During the pre-reading segment, Teacher Yang designed an EDB drag-and-drop game where students dragged Spring Festival–related elements down under the digital blackboard (elements for other holidays were locked in advance by the teacher and couldn't be dragged), activating students' existing vocabulary and preparing them for the while-reading segment. Students eagerly took part, and the classroom atmosphere was very lively.

Teacher Yang used the scan-to-upload image feature to upload practice questions students had completed on paper task sheets onto the digital blackboard, then dragged out pre-made whiteboard content from the side of the digital blackboard, using an interactive classroom intro to help students connect new material with what they already knew.

**Beijing Xinxian Hutong Primary School — "Double Ninth Festival — the Character 'Nine' (九)"**

After small-group inquiry, Teacher Hu had students arrange the two strokes of the character "九" (nine) on a gold-colored grid, focusing on issues like incorrect stroke placement and where long/short strokes should land. The teacher then took screenshots of students' arrangements to generate examples of the character's stroke features for comparison and understanding, tackling the lesson's key difficulty — the character's slanting posture and tightened center. The greatest value of this segment was that the teaching difficulty emerged from the students themselves rather than being preset by the teacher, truly making students the center of the activity.

Based on the characters "重阳" (Double Ninth) shown in the pre-made whiteboard content, students found the matching "九" in that font to complete a character-collecting exercise. By observing the stroke features of different calligraphic styles, students gained a deeper understanding of the Zhao-style regular script taught in this lesson. At the end of class, the teacher photographed and uploaded outstanding student work, turning it into home decor pieces — extending classroom learning beyond the classroom and reinforcing the moral value of respecting one's elders.

### 2.5 Real Teaching Case Studies

**Beijing No.1 Normal University Affiliated Primary School — "Volume and Units of Volume"**

Teacher Yang used ClassIn's built-in physics virtual lab feature during a math lesson — using a beaker-and-buoyancy experiment, students dropped small stones into the beaker, letting the rising water level give them an intuitive sense of the concept of volume, visualizing an abstract idea.

Teacher Yang used ClassIn's screen projection feature, having students operate ClassIn plus an iPad to directly measure the volume of the offline classroom and learn about volume that way.

**Beijing Xinxian Hutong Primary School — "Tick Tock"**

Teacher Lang designed a line-matching exercise, guiding students to complete the matching activity using the "straight line" pen tool. Students eagerly took part, and the classroom atmosphere was very lively — enriching the format of classroom practice and boosting students' interest and engagement.

Teacher Lang used the whiteboard's layer design feature, guiding students to drag cartoon images to find the picture matching each word, helping them more intuitively learn the English word "T-shirt" and practice answering special questions starting with "How many."

**Beijing No.109 Middle School — "Conservation of Mechanical Energy"**

Features used: iPad-controlled PPT, iPad same-screen annotation, photo upload, countdown timer, physics virtual lab

**Qingdao Taiping Road Primary School, Shandong — "24-Hour Time Notation"**

During the in-class practice segment, Teacher Liu combined EDB whiteboard content with teaching tools to have students classify different ways of expressing time. Teacher Liu rolled the dice for students, moving a rocket forward based on the number shown — enriching the format of classroom practice and boosting students' interest and engagement.

Once the rocket reached its destination, students had to judge whether the time shown on the star used 24-hour or 12-hour notation. After students made their judgment, the teacher dragged the star into the corresponding category box.
