### 5.1 Method 1

If ClassIn's installer prompts you for authorization, tap "Allow" and the system automatically jumps to the "Install unknown apps" settings screen — enable the "Allow installing apps" toggle there (a gray toggle means it's off, a green toggle means it's on).

### 5.2 Method 2

If the ClassIn installer prompts for authorization but there's no direct jump button, you can open your phone's Settings directly, go to Apps, search for "ClassIn," go into ClassIn's app info, go to Permissions, tap "Install other apps," and check "Allow."

## Optimizing Device Performance and Network (Computer/Phone/Tablet)

# I. Optimizing Device Performance

### 1. Desktop Computer

Restart your computer before class — a computer left running for a long time without restarting can become sluggish.

Exit any antivirus or security software on your computer, since this kind of software uses up memory and can make your computer sluggish. Common examples on the market include 360 Total Security, Rising Antivirus, Tencent PC Manager, 2345 Safeguard, Kaspersky, McAfee, and other mainstream antivirus/security software.

Close every app besides ClassIn — having too many apps open can use up your computer's memory and cause sluggishness.

### 2. Laptop

Add a cooling pad underneath your laptop — especially in summer, when high ambient temperatures combined with prolonged high-temperature operation can cause the CPU to throttle and the laptop to become sluggish.

### 3. Phone and Tablet

Restart your phone or tablet before class.

Close every other app running in the background besides ClassIn (how to do this: swipe up from the bottom edge of the phone screen to open the "recent apps list," then press and hold the app you want to close and swipe it up to close it).

# II. Optimizing Your Network

If a teacher or student experiences audio/video out of sync, an intermittent camera feed, laggy network, slow loading when opening cloud drive courseware, or a sudden classroom disconnection during class, this may be caused by a poor network signal. Try the following solutions to improve it:

Close programs or websites on your computer that use up network bandwidth. For example: QQ Music, games, video streaming sites, or files currently downloading — we recommend temporarily closing or exiting these.

Disable your computer's security software. Some antivirus programs or security suites throttle your network — we recommend disabling them before class. Common antivirus/security programs include 360 Total Security, Rising Antivirus, Tencent PC Manager, 2345 Safeguard, Kaspersky, McAfee, and others.

Avoid running high-bandwidth activities at the same time. On the same network, if multiple users are simultaneously doing bandwidth-heavy activities like streaming video or gaming, it can slow down everyone else's network speed.

Disable your computer's firewall, or add ClassIn to the firewall's allowlist, to prevent it from restricting your network connection.

Periodically restart your router, unplugging it for a few minutes before powering it back on — this helps maintain a stable network connection.

If you're taking class or studying on a school campus, we recommend contacting your school's network administrator to have any network speed limits on your computer removed.

Use your phone's mobile data. If your Wi-Fi is unstable, you can turn on your phone's mobile data, connect the phone to your computer via USB cable, then go to "Settings" on your phone, into "Personal Hotspot," and enable "USB Internet Sharing."

Move your laptop closer to your router before class — too much distance can weaken the signal between your device and the router, affecting connection quality.

If your laptop has an Ethernet port, you can connect via a network cable instead of Wi-Fi for a more stable, faster connection.

### 3. Phone or Tablet

Move your phone or tablet closer to your router before class — too much distance can weaken the signal between your device and the router, affecting connection quality.

If your phone is connected to Wi-Fi, try disconnecting from Wi-Fi and using mobile data instead.

If you're using a tablet, try connecting to a mobile hotspot shared from a phone.

Check whether your phone has good network coverage where you are — if the signal is weak, move to an area with better signal.
