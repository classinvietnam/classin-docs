## 2. Turning Dual Cameras On/Off

### What is dual-camera mode?
Dual-camera mode refers to a teacher choosing "1v1 Dual Camera" mode when creating the class session. In this mode, every on-stage user in the classroom can display two camera feeds at once, enabling multi-angle demonstration and teaching. This mode offers a more professional filming experience for teaching, letting students observe the subject more clearly and improving learning outcomes.

### When to use this
In teaching scenarios, dual-camera mode is commonly used for art classes, instrument lessons, calligraphy classes, arts-and-crafts classes, and similar. For example, in a piano lesson, both the teacher and student can turn on dual-camera mode at once — one camera captures the teacher's or student's face, and the other captures their hands on the instrument. This lets students see the teacher's fingering technique more clearly, and lets the teacher notice how the student is playing in time to give more targeted guidance.

Dual-camera mode only supports "1v1 class types" — that is, only one lead teacher and one student can show their cameras on the platform at the same time. The classroom will then show two cameras each for the lead teacher and the student. Note: to use dual-camera mode, the user's device must have two cameras in order to properly turn on two camera feeds in the classroom. This means users on phone or tablet cannot enter a dual-camera classroom for class.

### Notes
Teachers in a dual-camera mode classroom cannot use the "Auxiliary Camera" tool from the teaching tools.

1. Click the square icon in the top-right corner of the classroom.
2. Go to Primary Settings to turn the first camera on/off.
3. Go to Secondary Settings to turn the second camera on/off.

## 4. Switching Cameras

When a user takes class on phone or tablet with the camera turned on, it defaults to capturing what's behind the device. Users can switch from the rear camera to the front camera in classroom settings.

In the ClassIn classroom, "front-facing" and "rear-facing" are the two common camera orientations. Front-facing means the camera points toward the person using the device; rear-facing means it points away, toward what's behind the device. Normally, when entering the classroom, the camera should point at yourself to ensure a correct video feed. If you find the video orientation is reversed after entering class, try adjusting the front/rear camera setting.

## 6. Camera Mirroring

There are two common scenarios in teaching where you'd need to consider toggling "Mirror Mode."

**Scenario one:** if a user is showing text or an illustration in a book via their camera, and other classroom members see the text or image reversed, the presenter needs to turn off camera "Mirror Mode" so it displays correctly.

**Scenario two:** while taking class, a user may notice that their own body movements appear reversed when they watch their own camera feed. Some users find this disorienting, in which case they can turn on camera "Mirror Mode" so their movements match what they see in their camera. Once this mode is on, the user's actual movements and the movements shown in their camera will match direction. Toggling camera mirror mode appropriately in these situations helps users present themselves more effectively.

1. Turn Mirror Mode on/off in the top-right corner.

## 7. Camera Virtual Background

Users on computer can use a virtual background for their camera.

The uploaded local image for a virtual camera background must be in PNG format.

Up to 10 local images can be uploaded; there's no size requirement for local images, and you can resize them after uploading.

Images larger than 2MB will have their resolution compressed to 0.6x.

Setting a virtual camera background is not currently supported on phone or tablet.

1. Go to Virtual Background.
2. Set the camera background.

## 8. Moving the Camera Window

Teachers can drag or double-click their own or a student's camera to reposition it.

**Double-clicking a camera:** the teacher double-clicks their own or a student's camera to enlarge it from the on-stage seats onto the main whiteboard; double-clicking it again returns it to the on-stage seats.

**Dragging a camera:** the teacher drags their own or a student's camera from the on-stage seats onto the main whiteboard, and can drag it back to the on-stage seats too. Dragging a camera while holding Ctrl keeps its size unchanged as it moves from the on-stage seats to the whiteboard.

#### Computer/Phone/Tablet
1. The teacher double-clicks or drags their own or a student's camera onto the main whiteboard.

## 9. Resizing the Camera Window

Teachers can resize their own or a student's camera on the main whiteboard.

**Method 2:** while a camera is on the main whiteboard, the teacher can click and hold the camera and drag it toward the bottom-left or bottom-right corner of the classroom to shrink it.

## 4. Background Noise Reduction

When a computer user speaks using their microphone in the classroom, if other classroom members hear a lot of background noise mixed into their voice, the user experiencing the noise issue can enable "Background Noise Reduction" in classroom settings, reducing interference and improving voice call quality. If the user is on phone or tablet, they need to enable "Microphone AI Noise Reduction" in App Settings → Advanced Settings outside the classroom.

Once a teacher enables noise reduction, Music Mode is automatically disabled and won't take effect; conversely, if the teacher enables Music Mode, students' noise reduction won't take effect either.

1. Turn Background Noise Reduction on/off.

![](https://cofile.eeo.cn/res-store%2F8eb3aff93fdc2bd06e604e340b82c56ef12115f270e7bb92458cfb02d4f92752_99953?q-sign-algorithm=sha1&q-ak=AKIDJQGsEOK2TfbFdEZifMnxrWx85mpdHj6H&q-sign-time=1773936000;1774540800&q-key-time=1773936000;1774540800&q-header-list=host&q-url-param-list=&q-signature=e97c71efcdc41b343e2ca15d89699de003327ab4)

## 5. Music Mode

### What is Music Mode?
"Music Mode" in the classroom is a commonly used setting. Once a teacher enables "Music Mode," teachers and students in the classroom can experience a more authentic reproduction of instrument sound, making it come through more clearly, naturally, and true-to-life for every classroom member.

"Lead teacher" and "co-teacher."

Once a teacher on computer enables "Music Mode," to achieve a more authentic musical effect, both background noise reduction and echo suppression in the classroom will be disabled (the teacher's background noise reduction is turned off, and students enabling their own background noise reduction or AI noise reduction has no effect). Phone and tablet do not have Music Mode.

1. Turn Music Mode on/off.

## 3. Switching Speakers

Try switching speakers in the classroom in the following situations:

**Multiple speaker devices connected:** for users taking class on computer, if multiple speaker devices are connected simultaneously — for example, Bluetooth headphones, a wired headset, and speakers — you can switch speakers in the classroom to choose which device the sound comes from.

**No sound from an HDMI external display:** for users taking class on a laptop, if you've connected an external display via HDMI but find you can't hear any sound during class, and you've confirmed both the classroom's and the computer system's speaker volume are turned up, this may be because the external display doesn't have speakers, while the computer's audio output is set to the display. In this case, you can switch speakers in the classroom back to the laptop's built-in speakers.

**Wanting sound from an HDMI-connected TV:** for users taking class on a laptop, if you've connected to a TV via HDMI and want sound to come from the TV, you can switch speakers in the classroom to the TV's speaker.

Mac computers do not support switching the classroom speaker — the classroom's default speaker is the computer's local speaker.

Switching speakers is not supported on phone or tablet.

1. Under Select Speaker, choose the speaker to use.

## 3. Fixing Audio/Video

All members.

When a user turns on their microphone in the classroom but there's no sound when speaking, they can try using the "Hardware Fix" feature in the classroom.

When a user's microphone option keeps automatically switching back to muted after they turn it on in the classroom, they can try using the "Hardware Fix" feature in the classroom.

When a user can't hear any other classroom member speaking, they can try using the "Hardware Fix" feature in the classroom.

When a user's camera video isn't displaying properly, they can try using the "Hardware Fix" feature in the classroom.

We recommend restarting your computer once the fix is complete.

Phone and tablet don't have a "Hardware Fix" feature in the classroom — if you run into the issues above, try restarting your phone or tablet instead.

Mac computers do not have a "Hardware Fix" feature.

1. Click Hardware Fix in the bottom-left corner.
2. Fix the relevant feature based on the symptom.
