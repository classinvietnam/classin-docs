## Blended Scenarios and Core Competency Teaching

## I. Concept Overview

### 1.1 What Is Blended Teaching?

Implementing core-competency curriculum teaching is a challenging learning theme (unit course) that requires active student participation and proactive learning, sparking intrinsic motivation and developing higher-order thinking, and calls for deep engagement with a wide range of learning tasks and subject-based practice activities.

A process like this inevitably won't happen within a single classroom space alone. In terms of the space where teaching and learning happen, this necessarily becomes a ubiquitous learning space that combines in-school classroom teaching with independent, out-of-school learning and practice; it necessarily becomes a blended learning space, supported by informatization and digital tools, that merges the physical world with the virtual world; and it necessarily becomes a learning space supported by modern technology, suited to a comprehensive, well-rounded process of learning and development.

## II. How to Put It into Practice

Adapted from Professor Yang Xiaozhe's "Transformations in Learning Environments and Methods Under New Technology"

### 2.1 Seven Common Problems with Integrating Technology Into Teaching Today

**Stance:** it's usually the teacher using the technology, not the student

**Unit:** technology use tends to be overly fragmented, often applied to a single segment within an activity rather than to learning as a whole

**Orientation:** knowledge or functionality gets broken down (into points) rather than integrated (with technology blending into the full experience of learning, life, and society)

**Goal:** stuck at basic knowledge and skills, with separated three-dimensional objectives, rather than being oriented toward competency goals

**Focus:** more attention goes to teachers using technology to present information better, rather than to helping students process information (learning how to learn)

**Attention:** more focus on overall achievement, overlooking individual differences

**Tension:** excessively chasing or overstating smart technology, while ignoring the basic consensus, basic conditions, and basic applications of the current situation

### 2.2 Seven Effective Strategies for Blended Online-Offline Teaching

Use a learning management system to weave technology into the complete learning process

Design at least some portion of learning to happen online

Make every offline class also joinable online

Provide a variety of learning resources to support unit-based learning

Choose tools suited to promoting student cognition and collaboration

Use a variety of different teaching organization methods as needed

Design personalized learning informed by data feedback

### 2.4 How to Use Technology in the Classroom?

**Three dimensions of technology in instructional design:**

Multi-dimensional context building: deep learning grounded in authentic scenarios

Interactive inquiry tools: competency-based unit design

Personalized progress tracking: staged assessment of problem-solving

Build or use a variety of resource formats — text, images, video, models, datasets, virtual scenarios, and so on — to highlight the unit's authentic context, giving students vivid, intuitive, thought-provoking learning materials. Let students immerse themselves in context from multiple angles, strengthening their ability to identify, analyze, and solve problems.

### 2.9 Classroom Practice

**Beijing No.1 Normal University Affiliated Primary School — "Classification and Organization"**

Teacher Gao used ClassIn's whiteboard layer feature to turn shapes of different colors and forms into movable images, guiding students to freely drag the images to learn about classification and grouping concepts. Since the teacher didn't specify a classification standard, students' dragging actions ended up visualizing their own thinking.

The teacher used ClassIn's split-screen feature to show the whiteboard and PPT together — first guiding students to count different fruits on the whiteboard, then using the PPT to have students trigger the matching fruit count and write the answer in Arabic numerals. Using the same teaching materials across different classes, the teacher got a variety of randomly generated student responses.

**Beijing Xinxian Hutong Primary School — "Bringing in the New Year"**

During the pre-reading segment, Teacher Yang designed an EDB drag-and-drop game where students dragged Spring Festival–related elements down under the digital blackboard (elements for other holidays were locked in advance by the teacher and couldn't be dragged), activating students' existing vocabulary and preparing them for the while-reading segment. Students eagerly took part, and the classroom atmosphere was very lively.

Teacher Yang used the scan-to-upload image feature to upload practice questions students had completed on paper task sheets onto the digital blackboard, then dragged out pre-made whiteboard content from the side of the digital blackboard, using an interactive classroom intro to help students connect new material with what they already knew.

## III. Lesson Example and Method

Using "Area of a Circle" as an example

**Thoughts during lesson prep:**

Learning how to calculate a circle's area and its applications. Building on foundational knowledge of the concept of area and calculating polygon area, students explore the formula for calculating a circle's area.

How can a comprehensive application problem be framed in an authentic context to give students room to explore? How should instruction be designed to develop students' mathematical thinking methods, such as transformation and limits?

How can students develop the ability to formulate and solve problems, and appreciate the beauty of mathematical exploration and practice during self-directed learning...

**Learning Activity — Discussion**, pre-class discussion on "What circles exist in everyday life?"

Teachers can publish a discussion activity to spark student thinking and let students communicate independently.

Teachers can see every student's discussion contributions, making it easier to provide personalized guidance and more flexibly adjust learning content and teaching methods. Students and teachers can comment and like each other's posts, creating good interaction and giving everyone a chance to voice their views.

**Learning Activity — Quiz**, a pre-class quiz to help students review the concept of area and how to calculate polygon area; a post-class quiz to check learning outcomes

Teachers can give students a pre-class quiz to help them review what they've learned and identify gaps, while also letting the teacher gauge students' current level to plan the upcoming lesson appropriately and improve classroom efficiency.

Quizzes can also be assigned individually, used to check students' understanding after class.

**Learning Activity — Class Session**, teaching the lesson's content, flexibly using in-classroom mini-tools to enrich classroom activities.

Class sessions support real-time audio/video interaction, and can happen online, offline, or in a blended, mixed format.

Teachers can use in-classroom mini-tools — such as the answer clicker and small blackboard — to enrich classroom activities and improve learning outcomes.

**Learning Activity — Learning Materials**, syncing the class task file, with students completing the class learning task according to the file's instructions (openable within the classroom)

Teachers can use Learning Materials to publish a learning guide, letting students understand the learning plan for each upcoming module before class. Teachers can also publish pre-class materials, guiding different student groups toward purposeful, personalized learning, improving the effectiveness of preview and deep learning.

Learning Materials support audio recordings, cloud drive files, local files, web links, and other formats, for students to study independently, rewatch repeatedly, and download anytime.

**Learning Activity — Assignment**, an optional assignment exploring the area of a circle, for students with capacity to go further to attempt

Teachers can assign homework to help students think more deeply and understand course content, developing their thinking and expression skills.

**Learning Activity — Recorded Course**, a pre-class micro-lecture for the second class period, "The Math in Pizza"

Teachers can publish supplementary knowledge and extension content in the form of a recorded course, boosting students' initiative and independence in learning. The system automatically tracks each student's viewing progress, so teachers always have a clear picture of how students are doing.

**Pre-Setting a "Competency Skills" Evaluation Plan**

Before creating a new learning activity, teachers can first set up competency skill groups, adding the relevant skill indicators and indicator descriptions.

When creating a teaching activity, they can then quickly link it to competency skills and grade based on student completion — the system automatically generates a competency evaluation result.

**Process-Based Evaluation and Competency Evaluation**

During course implementation, both teachers and students can see process-level learning data, helping teachers better understand students' actual performance and offer timely support. At the same time, students can better understand their own learning status, identify and fill gaps, and improve their learning outcomes.

**Reading and Learning — Teacher Growth Exchange**

*"New Plan, New Standards, New Journey"* is a book interpreting the new curriculum standards, written by professors from East China Normal University.

To help teachers better understand the new curriculum standards and apply them in teaching, our company organized a shared-reading group activity — outstanding teachers were nominated by basic-education schools within a set quota to take part.

The activity ran a kickoff session, a closing session, and expert sharing sessions through class sessions, with teachers holding small-group discussions.

Teachers' questions and desired sharing topics were gathered through Discussion.

Debate competitions and "roast" sessions were explained and demonstrated via recorded courses.

Assignments and quizzes were used to check teachers' understanding.

Accumulated scores were used to recognize outstanding individual teachers.

Through the LMS, dialogue was made possible between basic-education teachers and new-curriculum-standard experts — teachers broke through spatial boundaries to form study groups and exchange experience. After the activity, teachers used the LMS again to pass on what they'd learned in the shared-reading group to teachers at their own schools.
