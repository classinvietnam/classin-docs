## WeChat Official Account Notifications

## I. Linking a WeChat Official Account

### Applies to
Everyone.

### When to use this
Once a user links the ClassIn WeChat official account, they can receive class notifications and notifications about LMS learning activities in their class via WeChat, even without logging into the ClassIn app or enabling ClassIn app notifications — so they never miss anything important.

### Usage Notes
Once linked, ClassIn will send reminder messages to teachers and students via the WeChat official account before class starts. Specific timing:

**(1) Paid ClassIn class notifications:**
- Teachers: notified 20 minutes before class starts.
- Students: notified 10 minutes before class starts.

**(2) LMS activity notifications:**
- When a teacher publishes an LMS learning activity, or grades, sends a reminder for, or requests a correction on a student's already-submitted LMS learning activity, the student will receive a WeChat push notification.
- When a student submits an LMS learning activity, the teacher will receive a WeChat push notification.

Through these targeted notifications, ClassIn ensures every teacher and student gets timely updates on important class and learning activity information.

### Notes
For free-tier classrooms, teachers, co-teachers, students, and auditors are all notified 5 minutes before class.

The WeChat official account can send up to 1 million pushes per day; beyond that, further pushes are blocked.

Course or lesson names longer than 24 characters will also be blocked from notifications.

### Steps
1. In WeChat, tap the magnifying glass in the top-right corner.
2. Search for "ClassIn Official Account" and tap it in the results.
3. Go into the ClassIn official account.
4. Tap Follow.
5. Tap Link Account.
6. Tap Confirm Link.
7. Enter your phone number, email, or ClassIn account and password to link.
8. When a class or LMS activity reminder is due, it will show up in the WeChat official account.

## Learning Materials

## I. What Are "Learning Materials"?

In teaching practice, teachers can upload preview or review materials to the Learning Materials section so students can preview before class or review afterward. This enriches teaching content, improves teaching outcomes, and gives students a more complete, organized set of learning resources.

## II. Creating "Learning Materials"

### Applies to
Head teachers and teachers.

### Usage Notes
After entering the class page, teachers can post learning materials under the course tab. Learning materials support several types of attachments: images, voice recordings, cloud drive files, local files, and website links. File limits: up to 15 image attachments, up to 15 recording attachments, up to 15 video attachments, and the combined total of local files, cloud drive files, and links cannot exceed 50; each video or file must be under 500MB. When uploading from a computer, you can drag and drop images and files directly into the learning material.

**(1) Settings available when creating "Learning Materials":**

| Setting | Function |
| --- | --- |
| Time | Set the start and due time for the learning material. |
| Publish to | Publish the learning material to a specific class, to some group members or specific students within a class, or to multiple classes at once. |
| Teacher | Assign the activity's teacher to receive related notifications. |
| Course | Choose which course the learning material belongs to. Defaults to the current course. |
| Unit Topic | Choose which unit topic the learning material belongs to. If not selected, it goes into the default unit. |
| Allow students to download attachments | Once enabled, students can download the learning material's attachments. |
| Allow newly joined students to view | Once enabled, students who join the class before the learning material activity ends can also view it. |
| Unlock conditions | Add prerequisite activities that must be completed. If unlock conditions are added, students must complete them before starting this learning material activity. |

### Notes
If "a student was removed from the class by a teacher," "the class was dissolved," or "a student left the class voluntarily," whether the departed student can still view file attachments in learning materials depends on the class setting for allowing students who left the class or whose class was dissolved to view course content.

### Steps

**Computer/tablet**

2. Create a new course, or open an existing one, in the top-left corner.
3. Click New.
4. Create Learning Material.

For materials that have ended, the content can no longer be edited, and the following settings can no longer be changed: time, publish to, teacher, course, allow newly joined students to view, and unlock conditions.

## Unit Topics

## I. What Is a "Unit Topic"?

In course design, we often organize teaching content around a central theme, forming a connected series of teaching activities that together span several class sessions — we call this a "unit." Note: "unit" here doesn't fully correspond to a "chapter" in a traditional textbook, and "topic" is not simply a textbook unit or chapter title either.

## II. Creating a "Unit Topic"

### Applies to
Head teachers and teachers.

### Usage Notes
A unit topic can include a variety of teaching/learning activities, such as class sessions, recorded courses, assignments, quizzes (exam papers), answer sheets, check-ins, discussions, learning materials, and AI Speaking Cards. Through this range of activities, students can gain a full, coherent understanding of related knowledge within a single topic framework.

### Steps

**Computer/tablet**
1. Go to Home.
2. Enter the class.
3. Create a new course, or open an existing one.
4. Click New.
5. Select Unit Topic.
6. Add the unit topic's title and description.
7. Publish directly, or save as a draft.

## VI. Sorting "Units and Activities"

Applies to everyone.

### When to use this
When a class has many unit topics or learning activities, teachers or students can switch sort order to more easily find what they need.

Course sorting supports "ascending order" and "descending order."

Ascending order sorts topics/activities by creation time, from oldest to newest.

Descending order sorts topics/activities by creation time, from newest to oldest.

Items that can be sorted: unit topics, class sessions, recorded courses, assignments, quizzes, answer sheets, discussions, learning materials, check-ins, AI Speaking Cards, and so on.

1. Open the filter panel.
2. Sort ascending or descending, or filter by activity type, teacher, or completion status.

## VII. Filtering Activities

There are three ways to filter learning activities, to suit different needs:

**Condition filtering:** filter by a specific condition — for example, a teacher can filter all teaching activities related to them; a student can filter all teaching activities under a specific teacher, or filter by not-yet-started, in-progress, or ended activities. Students can also use this to filter learning activities they still need to complete, or filter by "unit topic" or "competency," and so on. Combining multiple filter conditions lets you pinpoint exactly the learning activities you need.

**Type filtering:** filter by activity type — for example, class sessions (lessons, live sessions), recorded courses, assignments, check-ins, quizzes (exam papers), answer sheets, discussions, learning materials, AI Speaking Cards, and so on. This helps users quickly find a specific type of teaching/learning activity.

**Keyword search:** search for teaching/learning activities by entering a keyword, to quickly find relevant activities.

## VIII. Grades

Applies to head teachers, teachers, and official class students.

Every course within a class has a grades page. On the teacher side, it includes learning activities and competency models; clicking on an official student navigates to that student's course report. On the student side, clicking on grades navigates to your own course report, and switching units shows that unit's report.

When a teacher views the grades page on a phone, only the student's overall grade and progress are shown; clicking on a grade navigates to their course report, and switching units shows that unit's report.

**Learning Activities:** teachers can view the overall grades and participation of students across graded activities in the selected course, filterable by unit and activity type; on computer, the current page's content can be exported, and the overall grading scheme can be managed and changed in Evaluation Settings; clicking on a student navigates to that student's unit report — Learning Analytics.

**Competency Models:** teachers can view student competency model results for activities that use grading rubrics within the selected course, and can search by student and by competency model; on computer, the current page's content can be exported, and competency models can be managed in Evaluation Settings; clicking on a student navigates to that student's unit report — Competency Analysis.

## IX. Unit Reports

A unit report is a summary and evaluation of a student's learning at a given stage, made up of two main pages: Learning Analytics and Competency Analysis.

The Learning Analytics page includes a summary of unit activities (activity completion, class attendance, etc.), competency analysis, the learning process (completion status of every activity in the unit, with clicking through to activity details or activity reports), and teacher comments. The Competency Analysis page visually presents a competency map and list based on the selected competency model.

Both Learning Analytics and Competency Analysis can be shared: on computer/tablet, you can share to a ClassIn class/friend, copy the link, or share via WeChat QR code; on phone, you can additionally share to Moments, Weibo, or QQ.

1. Find the unit topic, click "···," then click Unit Report.
2. View the Learning Analytics page.

## Discussion

## I. What Is "Discussion"?

"Discussion" refers to creating a discussion group within a class. In this group, all students and teachers in the class can freely ask questions, share opinions and views, explain concepts, or post their thoughts. This kind of discussion group creates an open, inclusive atmosphere that encourages students to think and express themselves actively, while also giving teachers an important window into students' thinking and learning needs. In addition, by exchanging ideas with classmates, students can share knowledge and experience with each other, promoting mutual learning. This kind of interaction and collaboration helps spark students' enthusiasm and motivation to learn, and helps build their critical thinking and problem-solving skills.

## II. "Discussion" vs. "Group Discussion" — What's the Difference?

ClassIn has two discussion features. One is the discussion feature outside the classroom, within a class — once created, any class teacher or student can take part. The other is inside the classroom, called "Group Discussion," which mainly splits students into several small groups to discuss within the classroom, and also lets the teacher join in on discussions across different groups.

## III. Creating a "Discussion"

### Applies to
Head teachers and teachers.

### Usage Notes
When creating a new discussion, teachers can type text and upload various attachments to the discussion content — images, recordings, cloud drive or local files, and links. File limits: up to 15 image attachments and 15 recording attachments; the combined total of local files, cloud drive files, and links cannot exceed 15; each file must be under 500MB.

When uploading discussion attachments from a computer, you can drag and drop images and files directly into the discussion.

**Settings available when creating a "Discussion":**

| Setting | Function |
| --- | --- |
| Time | Set the discussion's start and end time. |
| Grading | Set the discussion's grading scheme and points. |
| Publish to | Publish the discussion to a specific class, to some group members or specific students within a class, or to multiple classes at once. |
| Teacher | Assign the activity's teacher to receive related notifications. |
| Course | Choose which course the discussion belongs to. Defaults to the current course. |
| Unit Topic | Choose which unit topic the discussion belongs to. If not selected, it goes into the default unit. |
| Allow students to see each other's grades | Once enabled, students can see each other's grades. |
| Allow newly joined students to participate | Once enabled, students who join the class before the discussion ends can also take part in the already-published discussion. |
| Unlock conditions | Add prerequisite activities that must be completed. If unlock conditions are added, students must complete them before starting this discussion activity. |

## IV. Editing a "Discussion"

Discussions can be edited, but what can be edited depends on the discussion's status.

For drafts and discussions that haven't started, "Course" cannot be changed; everything else can be edited.

For discussions currently in progress, content can be edited, but "Start Time" and "Course" in the settings cannot be changed.

For discussions that have ended, content can no longer be edited, and the following settings can no longer be changed: time, publish to, teacher, course, allow newly joined students to participate, and unlock conditions.

1. Go to the course.
2. Find the discussion, click "···," then select Edit.

When a teacher shares a discussion from a computer, they can share it to a ClassIn class or friend, or copy the link, or scan a WeChat QR code with their phone to share to WeChat friends or groups.

## IX. Students Taking Part in a "Discussion"

Applies to students.

Students can view and take part in discussions from their to-dos or within the class.

The comment section has "Hottest" and "Newest" tabs. Within the comment section:

Students can like, post, and reply to (their own or others') comments; comments support typed text and uploaded images, recordings, and audio/video files. File limits: up to 9 image attachments and 9 recording attachments, with the combined total of audio/video files also capped at 9; each file must be under 500MB. On computer, students can drag and drop image files directly into a comment.

Students can tap "Show only my comments" or "Show only this person's comments" after their own or someone else's comment; they can delete their own replies or comments; on phone, they can also copy a comment.

If a student is muted by a teacher, attempting to post a reply will show a message that they are (or have been) muted; while muted, they cannot like, post, or reply to comments.

In the Students area, students can see their own comments, replies, likes received, and grade; on computer or tablet, they can also share their own discussion report. If the teacher has enabled "Allow students to see each other's grades," students can also see other classmates' discussion activity.

Once a discussion ends, students can no longer post replies, like, or delete comments.

## X. Teachers Taking Part in a "Discussion"

After finding an in-progress or ended discussion in their to-dos or within the class, teachers can enter it to view, participate, and grade.

Teachers can like, post, and reply to (their own or others') comments; comments support typed text and uploaded images, recordings, and audio/video files. File limits: up to 9 image attachments and 9 recording attachments, with the combined total of audio/video files also capped at 9; each file must be under 500MB. On computer, teachers can also drag and drop image files directly into a comment.

Teachers can tap "Show only my comments" or "Show only this person's comments" after their own or someone else's comment. A head teacher can delete anyone's comment; a class teacher can delete their own and students' comments (but not the head teacher's); on phone, comments can also be copied.

Teachers can mute students — once muted, a student trying to post a reply will see a message that they are (or have been) muted, and cannot like, post, or reply to comments while muted.

In the Students area, teachers can see each student's comments, replies, likes received, and grade; on computer or tablet, they can also share a specific student's discussion report; on computer, they can also download the discussion content.

If grading is enabled for the discussion, teachers can grade a student both after their comment in the discussion area and in the Students area.
