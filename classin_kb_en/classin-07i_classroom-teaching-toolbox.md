## Teaching Toolbox

## I. Screen Share

## 1. Teacher Screen Share

### Applies to
Lead teacher, co-teacher.

### When to use this
"Screen Share" is a professional teaching tool in the classroom's teaching toolbox. Through the "Screen Share" feature in the ClassIn classroom, teachers can show teaching content in real time so students can watch and learn along, improving teaching outcomes and interactivity. Teachers can also remotely control a student's desktop, quickly resolving issues the student runs into, significantly improving the student's learning experience and teaching outcomes.

In ClassIn, screen sharing comes in two modes: "Teacher Screen Share" and "Student Screen Share." "Teacher Screen Share" refers to a teacher sharing their own computer screen content with students in real time. This is widely used in online education and blended teaching. Through screen sharing, teachers can show textbooks, courseware, demonstrate operating steps, or explain complex concepts, helping students watch and learn along in real time.

### Usage Notes
While sharing their screen, a teacher can share their computer desktop and sound, or choose to share a specific software window. During sharing, the teacher can still use the "Roster" and "Chat" tools, and can turn on their camera and microphone for better interaction with students.

In addition, teachers can use the "Pen," "Click Tool," "Select/Move," "Eraser," and "Text" tools to mark up and annotate the shared window. Teachers can also switch student permissions, specifically:

**Don't allow student control:** students can only watch — they cannot mark up or write in the teacher's shared window.

**Allow authorized students to control the view:** authorized students can collaborate with the teacher writing or drawing in the shared window.

**Allow authorized students to control the computer:** authorized students can use the "Click Tool" to control the teacher's computer.

### Notes
Phone and tablet only have "Screen Share" mode, which can only share what's on that device itself — students cannot draw on the teacher's device, etc.

"Teacher Screen Share" can only share a single software window separately if the system is Windows 8, Windows 10, or Mac; there's no limit on the maximum shared area (anything larger than 1920 x 1080 is automatically compressed down to 1920 x 1080).

Once cloud recording is used together with screen sharing in the classroom, the resulting playback shows the entire computer desktop — so the recorded playback footage isn't limited to just the window that was shared; other windows or the taskbar may also appear.

### Steps

#### Computer
1. Open the Teaching Toolbox on the right side of the classroom.
2. Select Screen Share.
3. Select Teacher Screen Share.
4. Click Share in the bottom-right corner to start sharing.

#### iPad
1. Open the Teaching Toolbox in the bottom-right corner of the classroom.
2. Click Start Live to begin sharing.

#### Phone/Android tablet
1. Select the Screen Share tool from the toolbox in the bottom-right corner of the classroom.
2. Click Start Sharing to begin sharing.

Lead teacher, co-teacher, the student whose screen is shared.

"Student Screen Share" refers to sharing what's on a student's computer, phone, or tablet screen with the rest of the classroom in real time. Phone and tablet cannot initiate "Student Screen Share."

1. Select Student Screen Share.
2. Click Send Request below to start sharing.

## II. Screen Projection

Everyone (only the lead teacher or co-teacher can open this tool; once opened, others can use it too).

During class, if a teacher or student wants to show what's on their phone/tablet or another computer's screen in the classroom, they can use the classroom's screen projection tool.

For projection to work, the projecting device and the receiving device must be on the same network.

**(1) Projecting from another computer to the classroom:** log into ClassIn on the projecting device, click Screen Projection in the left navigation, enter the receiving device's projection code and click Project, or select the receiving device's name to complete projection. The four green corners on the projecting device's screen indicate the projection area, which cannot be adjusted — the entire screen is always used.

**(2) Projecting from an Apple phone, tablet, or computer:** you can use the device's built-in mirroring/AirPlay feature and select the receiving device's name to complete projection; you can also use the ClassIn Screen Projection app, entering the projection code from the device to be projected and clicking Project, or selecting the receiving device's name.

**(3) Projecting from an Android phone or tablet:** you need to use the ClassIn Screen Projection app — enter the receiving device's projection code in the app and click Project, or select the receiving device's name to complete projection.

**(4) Projecting from other video apps that support projection:** open the video app, play the video, and tap the app's own projection button to select the receiving device's name to complete projection.

While using the screen projection tool, the top-right corner of the projection tool window offers more settings for this projection session:

**Sharing settings:** play the projecting device's sound through this device's speaker; share the projecting device's sound with everyone else in the classroom.

**Privacy settings:** allow receiving projections from all devices; once the projection window is open, allow receiving projections from all devices.

In addition, multiple devices can project into the classroom at once — once projection succeeds, the teacher can switch between viewing different devices' projected content in the bottom-right corner of the projection tool window.

Android phones and tablets need Android 6.0 or higher to download and install the ClassIn Screen Projection app; iPhones and iPads need iOS 12.0 or higher.

The screen projection tool can only display content from a phone/tablet or another computer onto the whiteboard — it cannot be used as a handwriting tablet. If a teacher wants to use their phone or tablet as a handwriting tablet, try the "Two-Device Classroom Entry" feature instead.

Screen projection in the classroom is only available to teachers taking class on computer.

1. Select Screen Projection.

## IV. Small Blackboard

## 1. Whiteboard Small Blackboard

Lead teacher, co-teacher, students.

"Small Blackboard" is a professional teaching tool in the classroom's teaching toolbox. It gives each student the equivalent of a personal mini blackboard, making it easy for them to complete tasks the teacher assigns on their own small blackboard. Small Blackboard has two modes: "Whiteboard Small Blackboard" and "Text Small Blackboard." "Whiteboard Small Blackboard" is mainly for drawing and writing. Once the teacher distributes small blackboards, students can create, draw, and write on their "Whiteboard Small Blackboard." Teachers can also enter any student's small blackboard at any time to collaborate with them directly.

| Stage | Details |
| --- | --- |
| Preparation | In this stage, the teacher can use the teaching tools in the bottom-left corner to set up the small blackboard's content; students cannot see this content yet. Once ready, click "Distribute" to move into the Answering stage. |
| Answering | In this stage, the teacher can use the search feature in the top-right corner of the small blackboard to search for a student by name, and can view any student's small blackboard at any time, including collaborating with them directly. The teacher can also switch the display mode above the small blackboard, choosing a single board, a 2x2 grid, or a 3x3 grid. In multi-board display mode, the teacher can click a student's small blackboard to jump straight into it. The teacher can also send students on/off stage, and click "Recall" to end the answering stage. |
| Grading | Once in the grading stage, the teacher can view any student's small blackboard (all students follow the teacher's view together), and can send students on/off stage and mark up or type on a student's blackboard. |

iPhone, Android phone, and Android tablet do not have the small blackboard tool, so teachers on these devices cannot initiate "Small Blackboard" for classroom members; however, students on phone or tablet can still normally take part in small blackboard activities.

The small blackboard's window size can be adjusted before it's published, but not after.

1. Select Small Blackboard.
2. Select Whiteboard.

## VI. Group Discussion

"Group Discussion" is a professional teaching tool in the classroom's teaching toolbox, designed to help teachers better organize classroom activities. By splitting students into different groups, students can discuss and learn together in breakout rooms, sparking interest and enthusiasm for learning. Teachers can adjust student groupings at any time, and can join in or silently monitor a group to understand their learning progress, improving teaching outcomes.

The classroom's "Group Discussion" and the class's "Discussion" feature are two entirely different features — a class discussion is a learning activity created under the class's course tab for all teachers and students in the class to discuss, while a classroom group discussion can only be created within the classroom, taking the form of multiple small breakout-room classrooms for students to discuss in.

On phone, you cannot initiate a group discussion — you can only receive one someone else has sent.

1. Select Group Discussion.

### Teacher Guide

**Copy classroom content:** to copy the content from the main classroom into every breakout room, check "Copy Classroom Content to Discussion Groups" in the bottom-left corner before starting the discussion.

**Synchronize audio/video:** once discussion starts, you can check "Sync Teacher Audio/Video to Discussion Groups" in the bottom-left corner, so students in every group can see the teacher's demonstration and explanation.

**Silently monitor a group discussion:** click "Silent Monitor" above a group window — the teacher can hear the discussion and see the video, but their own audio/video won't appear in that room.

**Join a group discussion:** click "Join Group" above a group window — the teacher's audio/video moves into that breakout room to interact normally with group members, and their audio/video will no longer appear in the main classroom.

**Show/hide the group window:** control this in the top-right corner.

**View group member info/adjust members:** hover the mouse over a group window to view member info; to adjust members, drag and drop them between groups.

### Student Guide

**Group leader permissions:** the group leader has the same permissions as a teacher — they can use the buttons below the camera and the tools in the teaching toolbox, and can operate the roster to send group members on/off stage, authorize them, mute their microphones, and so on. However, the group leader cannot award trophies, and cannot remove students from the classroom.

**Group member permissions:** group members have the same features as a regular classroom student, but are authorized by default.

### Post-Discussion Guide

**End discussion:** the teacher clicks "End Discussion" in the bottom-right corner to end the group discussion.

**Copy group content back to the main classroom:** to copy the content from a specific group room into the main classroom, click "Copy Discussion Group Content to Classroom" above that group's window. Note: this clears the main classroom's existing whiteboard content first.

**Bring a group's members on stage:** click "On Stage" above a group's window to bring all members of that group on stage.

## VIII. Buzzer

"Buzzer" is a professional teaching tool in the classroom's teaching toolbox. The buzzer can boost student participation and interaction in the classroom, spark students' enthusiasm to answer, and improve classroom atmosphere.

Once the teacher activates the buzzer and clicks "Start Buzzer," the buzzer immediately enters buzzer mode. The teacher's view shows "Buzzer Active," and the bottom of the screen shows the important stats "number of students taking part" and "total number of students online in the classroom." In students' view, the buzzer appears on the whiteboard and starts a 3-second countdown; once the countdown ends, the buzzer lands randomly at a spot on the whiteboard. Students can then start tapping the buzzer. The first student to buzz in successfully is automatically brought on stage to show their camera.

iPhone, Android phone, and Android tablet do not have the Buzzer tool.

During buzzing, if multiple students buzz in within 0.5 seconds of each other, the system randomly selects one of them as the winner; if no student buzzes in within 0.5 seconds, the system selects whichever student buzzed in first, based on timing order.

1. Select Buzzer.

In programming teaching scenarios, besides watching the teacher explain and write code, students also need hands-on practice, which usually needs to be completed independently in a web browser. ## XIII. Save/Share Whiteboard

The "Save/Share Whiteboard" tool is a professional teaching tool in the classroom's teaching toolbox. Teachers can use it while preparing lessons or teaching to save the whiteboard's content locally on their computer or to their cloud drive space.

When saving the whiteboard, the teacher can choose to save it locally or to the cloud drive. If saving locally, the teacher can simultaneously save it in multiple formats — EDB, PNG, and PDF. In addition, the teacher can choose whether to compress images in the whiteboard content, and set the whiteboard's background color. However, if saving to the cloud drive, only one format can be chosen at a time — EDB, PNG, or PDF.

Teachers can also share the whiteboard with a ClassIn friend or to a ClassIn class. Students can also get the whiteboard image by scanning the generated QR code.

When saving the whiteboard on phone or tablet, you cannot select a whiteboard background color.

Only computer users have the QR-code-to-generate-whiteboard-image feature — phone and tablet do not.

When a computer user saves the whiteboard locally, they can select edb, png, and pdf simultaneously, producing three different whiteboard file types locally; when saving to the cloud drive, only one file type can be selected at a time.

1. Select Save/Share Whiteboard.
2. Set the whiteboard background color in the bottom-left corner.
3. Select a save method or sharing method in the top-right corner.

Select Save Whiteboard.
Select a save method or sharing method on the right.

Select the Save Whiteboard tool from the toolbox in the bottom-right corner of the classroom.

## XIV. Video Wall

"Video Wall" is a professional teaching tool in the classroom's teaching toolbox. It gives teachers a way to view every student's camera feed in real time. If a teacher wants to observe all students' cameras in the classroom without bringing them on stage, the Video Wall lets the teacher quietly monitor each student's status during class, for better teaching and classroom management. In addition, the "Video Wall" can also be used for proctoring in the classroom.

The "Video Wall" control interface is only visible to the teacher — students cannot see it. From a student's view, they only see a small window in the bottom-left corner of the classroom showing their own camera. If a student sees this small window during class, it means the teacher is using the Video Wall feature.

In the "Video Wall" interface, the teacher can drag and drop to directly reposition students' cameras. Double-clicking a student's camera enlarges it to fill the entire Video Wall interface; double-clicking again restores its original size. To bring a student on stage or start a private video chat with them, the teacher can hover the mouse over the student's camera and click the quick-action buttons below it.

In addition, in the top-right corner of the Video Wall interface, teachers can set "Auto Page Turn," "Page Turn Interval," and "Layout," and can also take a group photo.

Tip: we recommend using the Video Wall with a dual-monitor setup — move the Video Wall to a secondary display so you can teach while keeping an eye on it at the same time.

**(1) Video Wall device requirements**

Phone and tablet do not have a Video Wall feature.

If a teacher is using two monitors for class, once Video Wall is opened, the Video Wall window automatically floats on the secondary display.

1. Select Video Wall.

## XX. VNC

### What is "VNC"?
VNC (Virtual Network Console) is a great remote control software tool.

ClassIn's VNC feature integrates a VNC client — teachers just need a separate computer set up as the VNC server, and can then use ClassIn's VNC feature to connect to that computer and use ClassIn's rich teaching features to teach through it.

Note: the computer with the VNC server installed needs a public IP address.

The classroom's "lead teacher" or "co-teacher" can use the VNC tool in the classroom.

### Configuration Notes
For the cloud server configuration you purchase, we recommend the following:

**(1)** If your class type is 1v6, the machine needs at least 2 cores and 4GB RAM. We strongly recommend choosing a bandwidth plan billed by actual usage, with a bandwidth requirement of at least 10Mbps.

**(2)** If your class type is 1v12, the machine needs at least 4 cores and 8GB RAM. We strongly recommend choosing a bandwidth plan billed by actual usage, with a bandwidth requirement of at least 20Mbps.

In addition, after purchasing a cloud server, you'll need to log into the cloud platform's management backend to modify the security policy and open the relevant ports (we recommend not using default ports like 3389, 5900, or 22 — port security needs to be configured separately).

Below are the steps for setting up a VNC server on Windows and Mac.

**2.1 Setting Up a VNC Server on Windows**

**(1)** First, download the VNC installer. Using TightVNC as an example: follow the installation wizard — choosing "Typical" installs both the client and server by default. To install only the client, choose "Custom" as shown below.

**(2)** In the TightVNC installation directory, launch tvnserver.exe to configure the VNC server: set a port number (default 5900) and password (note: the password cannot exceed 8 characters). This port number and password are what you'll need to enter when connecting via ClassIn's VNC feature.

## XXV. TI Store

During a livestream, teachers or teaching assistants can go to "Teaching Tools → TI Store" in the ClassIn livestream classroom, or academic teachers can go to "Class Product Management Settings" in the backend, to set a product's status to "Live for Sale," letting students purchase it directly while watching the livestream.

Product management and the TI Store are value-added services, available only to enterprise customers — please contact your dedicated account manager to enable them.

During a livestream, teachers, teaching assistants, and backend academic staff can only have 1 product listed at a time; once a new product is listed, the previous one is automatically taken down.

Once a student clicks to purchase, they'll be taken to their system browser to complete the transaction.

1. Select TI Store.
